02How it works

From first visit to final grant.

Allow about three months from the day we start drawing. Here is what happens, and who does what.

  1. Step 1

    Survey and drawings

    Kevin visits, measures the property and prepares the site location map, site layout plan, floor plans, elevations and sections the council needs.

  2. Step 2

    Public notices

    A notice goes in a newspaper and a site notice goes up at the property. The application has to be lodged within two weeks of the newspaper notice, and we time it so nothing lapses.

  3. Step 3

    Lodged with the council

    We lodge the application with Fingal County Council, or whichever council covers the property. For five weeks, anyone can make a submission on it.

  4. Step 4

    The decision

    The council normally decides within eight weeks. If it asks for further information, we prepare the response, and the council then has four weeks from receiving it to decide.

  5. Step 5

    Final grant

    After a decision to grant, there are four weeks for appeals. If there are none, the final grant issues and the work can start. We can check the build against the permission as it goes, so the certificate of compliance at the end is straightforward.

03Exempted development

Do you need planning permission at all?

Some work is exempted development, which means it doesn't need planning permission. The rules for houses are in the Planning and Development Regulations, and every condition has to be met. Miss one and the whole thing needs permission, which usually comes to light years later when the house is sold.

Common examples

  • Rear extensions: up to 40 square metres on the ground floor, counting earlier extensions, with at least 25 square metres of back garden left and the height and boundary rules met.
  • Garden rooms and sheds: up to 25 square metres behind the front of the house, not used to live in, with at least 25 square metres of garden left.
  • Front porches: up to 2 square metres, and more than 2 metres from a public road.
  • Attic conversions: work inside the existing roof is usually exempt. Dormer windows change the roof, so they usually need permission.

Kevin checks your plans against the rules before you build, and can give you a signed opinion of exemption for your records. When you want a formal answer from the council, we can apply for a Section 5 declaration for you.

The Building Regulations apply whether or not you need planning permission.
